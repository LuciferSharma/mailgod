The Ten
Commandments

Carved from a year of sending to a real 300,000-subscriber list. Every law below is a feature that ships in the box.

I

Thou shalt own thy list.

Your subscribers live in your MongoDB, not on a vendor's invoice. Export anything, anytime.

II

Thou shalt not pay per contact.

300 subscribers or 3 million, the license costs the same: nothing. MIT, forever.

III

Honor the reply.

Every email carries a tokenized Reply-To. Replies land in a shared inbox, threaded to the exact campaign and flow step.

IV

Know when to stop.

Flows exit on reply and hand the lead to a human. No more robots following up on a "yes".

V

Thou shalt not spam.

Per-subscriber frequency caps, a global rate limit, mailto List-Unsubscribe, CAN-SPAM footers.

VI

Send from many mouths.

A sender pool of your own SMTP mailboxes with daily caps, warmup, IMAP reply polling, and auto-pause when one gets sick.

VII

Keep the thread unbroken.

RFC 5322 threading so follow-ups land in the same conversation, from the same mailbox that started it.

VIII

Covet no redirector.

Click-tracking rewrites are disabled. Your links stay clean, and spam filters stay calm.

IX

Let the machines labor.

A built-in MCP server gives Claude and other agents 21 tools. Bulk sends are draft-first, so they can't blast your list by accident.

X

Judge by revenue, not opens.

Engagement scoring, an auto-advancing pipeline, Cal.com bookings, and weekly reports that diagnose themselves.

Agent-native

Your agents,
ordained.

Mailgod ships an MCP server. Plug it into Claude Code (or any MCP client) and your agent can prospect, segment, write and send, all through the same rules your humans follow.

It's safe by design. Bulk sends are draft-first, only confirm_send dispatches, there's a global kill-switch, and you can set hard recipient caps.
